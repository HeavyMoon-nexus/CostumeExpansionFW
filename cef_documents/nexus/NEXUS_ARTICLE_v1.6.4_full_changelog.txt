[center][size=6][b]What is in CEF 1.6.4[/b][/size][/center]
[center][size=4]Everything between 1.6.2 and 1.6.4, and why each of it happened[/size][/center]

[line]

The file page changelog says what changed. This says why, for the parts where the reason
is worth knowing.

If you are coming from 1.6.2, this release brings four versions at once. 1.6.2.1, 1.6.2.2
and 1.6.3 went to GitHub only and never went up here, so everything in them is in this
package.

[size=5][b]Before you install[/b][/size]

[b]One installer, instead of three downloads.[/b] The main mod, the NPC add-on and the VR
build were separate files. They are all in this one and it asks what you want.

[b]Two plugins come with this, and they are not the same kind of thing.[/b]
CostumeFW_BoxPool1.esp is part of the core install and is not optional: it carries the
extra box tokens that let one equipment slot hold more than one box. It is ESL, so it
costs no load-order slot. Do not remove it once a box sits on one of its tokens - that box
goes dormant, which is to say it keeps its contents and its name but cannot be worn or
edited until the plugin comes back.

CostumeFW_Abilities.esp is optional, but stat passthrough needs it. It is a normal ESP
rather than an ESL, so it does cost one of your 254 plugin slots. Leave it unticked and
your costumes look exactly the same; they just carry no stats, and CEF says so instead of
pretending. The reason it cannot be an ESL is below.

[b]Bonuses left over from before 1.6.3 are still in your save.[/b] This release stops new
ones happening. It cannot clean up what is already there, because CEF cannot tell its own
leftovers from a bonus another mod applied on purpose, and guessing wrong would take away
something you meant to have.

It will tell you what it thinks they are. Load a save made before 1.6.3 and CEF adds up
what the old mechanism would have been applying and shows it; [b]cef abilities legacy[/b]
prints the same again with the explanation. Values from conditional enchantments are
listed separately, because whether one of those was applying depends on what you were
doing at the moment you saved.

To clear one: take every box off, read [b]player.getavinfo health[/b] - or whatever the
box fortifies - and look at the number next to Perm. If it is not zero,
[b]player.modav health -<that number>[/b] cancels it. An NPC wearing a published costume
collects the same leftovers and they are much harder to notice.

[b]The MCM is still here[/b] and still works, with one gap: an equipment slot holding more
than one box gets no MCM page. Its pages are keyed by the slot number, so a slot with two
boxes names neither of them, and a page that quietly edited the wrong box would be worse
than no page. Use SMF for those. The MCM is still on its way out; I named 1.6.3 and then
1.6.4 for that and both slipped, so I am not naming a version this time. On VR, SKSE Menu
Framework needs [url=https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/183466]ImGui VR
Helper[/url].

[line]

[size=5][b]The stuck bonus, and why the spells are pre-made now[/b][/size]

Quit Skyrim while wearing a box and the bonus that box was giving you could stay on your
character permanently. It happened once per session, every session, and it added up. The
+180 Fortify Health that started this work was three sessions of one box.

Fixing it meant replacing how enchantment passthrough works rather than patching it. The
old mechanism built a spell while the game was running. A spell built that way does not
exist on the next launch, so your save drops the active effect that named it without ever
ending it, and the number it added stays with nothing able to take it back off. No amount
of care inside that approach avoids this - the spell has to still be there when the save
is read.

So the spells are pre-made. CostumeFW_Abilities.esp ships 1,024 empty ones and CEF fills
them in from a small file it keeps next to your settings, before your save is loaded. That
ordering is the whole fix, and it is also why that plugin cannot be an ESL: your save
points at fixed form IDs in it, and the light range cannot hold enough of them.

[b]Two pieces with the same enchantment were worth one.[/b] A box holding two Imperial
cuirasses, a heavy one and a light one carrying the same +40 Fortify Health, gave you +40.
CEF put both effects in one spell, and two entirely identical effects inside one spell
collapse into one.

This was measured rather than assumed. Two pieces with Resist Fire 40 behave the same way,
so it is not about Health. Two effects of the same kind with different magnitudes both
apply - Fortify Alteration 22 and 20 read 42. Two identical effects in different spells
both apply. So what the engine keys on is the whole content of an effect, within one
spell. Each piece has its own ability now, so nothing shares a spell and there is nothing
left to collapse.

[b]The pool can be extended.[/b] It was a single plugin of 1,024 abilities with no way to
add more. A second one is ESL and costs no load-order slot, and [b]cef abilities[/b] now
reports free space per plugin and which ability the next costume would take. Nothing to
re-capture when you add one: what a piece is worth has been re-read from your stored
original on every save load since 1.6.1, so a costume captured on an older version needs
nothing done to it.

[line]

[size=5][b]One slot, several boxes[/b][/size]

There were 27 box tokens, one per equipment slot, and if you wanted two different skirts
set up at once you could not have them - the slot was already spoken for. A slot holds
four boxes now, 108 in all. The SMF box list shows the token's name beside a box when its
slot has company, so two rows on the same slot are telling apart.

This is what CostumeFW_BoxPool1.esp is for, and why it ships in the core install rather
than as an option.

[line]

[size=5][b]Prepare for uninstall did not finish the job[/b][/size]

Two faults, both of them in 1.6.2 and in every version back to 1.6.0.

[b]It left your NPCs dressed.[/b] It gave back what your boxes held, took the tokens off
you and detached your own meshes, then stopped. Every published costume stayed on the NPC
wearing it, with its token, its carrier and its ability spell, which is the last thing
that should be sitting in the save of a mod you are about to delete. It recalls every
published costume and clears every NPC persist assignment now.

[b]And it left the items inside published costumes in storage.[/b] The same cleanup gives
back what a box holds and whichever persist items you have switched on. A published
costume is neither of those: publishing freezes the box into a snapshot, so its pieces sat
in CEF's hidden store with nothing left to walk over them, while the notification told you
it was safe to remove the mod. They come back now - the original where this save still has
it, tempering and enchantment included, and a plain copy where it does not.

If you are removing CEF on 1.6.2, unpublish your costumes back into their boxes first and
then run the cleanup, which does know about boxes.

[line]

[size=5][b]Publishing[/b][/size]

[b]A published costume did not count as owning the items in it.[/b] Publishing removes the
box it came from, and CEF then treated those items as belonging to nobody. You could
capture the same item into a second box, and since only one holder can display an item,
taking either one off stopped the other showing. Unpublishing ignored whether the items
actually went back into the box and deleted the costume anyway, so a piece that could not
be restored took the record of what the costume was with it. Unpublishing now checks the
whole restore before it dissolves the costume.

[b]Unpublishing could put a costume back on the head slot without saying so.[/b]
Publishing freed the slot the box was on, and if another box took that slot meanwhile the
costume had to come back somewhere else. CEF picked the lowest free slot, which is the
head, and equipping it made the head disappear. The first fix stopped automatic picks
landing on head or hair unless nothing else was free, and made CEF say so when a costume
came back somewhere new. This release removes the case instead: publishing holds the slot
in reserve rather than giving it away, so unpublishing puts the costume back where it came
from. If you published on 1.6.2 or 1.6.3 and then took the freed slot, the update finds
those costumes and gives each one another token on the same slot, so the outfit comes back
hiding what it always did.

[b]Disabling a costume's mod made its conditional enchantments unconditional.[/b] A "while
sneaking" bonus would sit on you while standing. CEF reads a captured piece's enchantment
from the stored original where it can, because that carries the conditions and the
duration, and the flat fallback carries neither. Publishing had the same defect on its own
side: a published costume rebuilt its stats from a frozen list that held only the effect
and its magnitude. Both build their stats the way a normal box does now.

[b]A published wig stopped hiding the NPC's own hair.[/b] A wig box occupies both the hair
slot and the long-hair slot, and the published copy only took the first, so the NPC's real
hair came through it.

[line]

[size=5][b]Costumes that would not come off[/b][/size]

[b]A box whose plugin you removed put its costume on you, and nothing could take it
off.[/b] None of CEF's own controls would remove it; only turning CEF off entirely did. A
persist item is marked by having no token at all, and a box whose token cannot be found
looked exactly the same from the inside, so it was treated as one. A dormant box now puts
nothing on you, and the box list says which box is dormant and why.

This one is in 1.6.2 as well. You cannot reach it there by uninstalling anything, since
1.6.2 has one plugin and it is required, but a hand-edited CEF_settings.json with a token
that does not resolve gets you the same stuck costume.

Two related guards came with it. A damaged CEF_settings.json can no longer produce boxes
on things that are not box tokens - a definition whose token does not resolve is kept and
quarantined with the reason, rather than dropped or half-loaded. And if a load would drop
most of your boxes at once, CEF refuses the load instead of writing the result back; the
file on disk is left exactly as it was. That one is there because a bad load used to be
able to make itself permanent.

[line]

[size=5][b]Crashes[/b][/size]

[b]A box name containing a percent sign could crash the game.[/b] Console output treated
the name as a format string, so "%s" or "%n" in a box label, a shape name or an NPC name
made it read arguments that were never there. Listing your boxes was enough to hit it.

[b]A preset with a wrong field type could take the game down.[/b] A hand-edited or
truncated preset file is now refused with a reason instead of trusted.

[line]

[size=5][b]NPCs[/b][/size]

[b]Reloading settings left NPCs stripped of their costumes.[/b] Anything that rebuilds
CEF's state unregisters every actor first, and only the player was being put back. This
did not need the settings file at all: adding one entry to the blocked list runs the same
rebuild, so blocking an unrelated item undressed an NPC standing in front of you.

[b]A blocked item still showed on NPCs.[/b] The blocked list is enforced where CEF
registers an item for display, and the NPC path was going straight to the model instead.
The item was also refused for the physics carrier, so what was drawn and what physics was
built from disagreed.

[line]

[size=5][b]Knowing what happened[/b][/size]

[b]CEF tells you when a captured original is gone.[/b] Disable a costume's plugin for a
single session and save, and Skyrim strips that plugin's items out of every container,
storage included. The box definition survives and the costume still displays; what is gone
is the captured original with its tempering and its player enchantment, and there was
nothing anywhere to tell you. Your save now carries a list of what storage held when it
was written and compares it on the next load: an item that has left is named in the log,
marked "lost" on the Recovery page, and reported on screen. It cannot be undone, but you
find out when it happens instead of months later from numbers that are wrong.

[b]The Recovery page says more about each row.[/b] Every row shows what last happened to
it, so a "lost" item stays marked after the session that found it. A row a box still holds
says when storage does not have it, and taking that one out of the box can only give you a
plain copy. A row whose plugin is not loaded says so and its Recover button is disabled
rather than offered, because recovery gives up before it reaches storage for an item it
cannot resolve. Recovering now also says which one you got, the captured original or a
plain copy. The page no longer costs frames while it is open.

[b]Three console commands.[/b] [b]cef av[/b] prints the actor values CEF is currently
changing and what each box contributes, which is the thing to read before deciding a
number looks wrong. [b]cef tokens[/b] lists every box token: which plugin it came from,
whether it is in use, reserved by a published costume or free, and which box has it.
[b]cef abilities[/b] reports the pool's free space.

[b]A startup check[/b] writes what it found to the log - which pools are installed,
whether any two boxes would build their physics carrier under the same name, whether any
box is holding a token a published costume also wants. It only looks; it changes nothing.

[b]The last two runs' logs are kept[/b] next to the current one, so a problem you noticed
after restarting is still there to read.

[line]

[size=5][b]Smaller things[/b][/size]

[b]Renaming a box no longer affects which tokens CEF thinks are free.[/b] Membership is a
hidden keyword now rather than the token's displayed name, which also means a translated
copy of CostumeFW.esp can no longer stop you making new boxes - that would have happened
to anyone running one.

[b]A box's tooltip could show another box's contents.[/b] With LoreBox installed, hovering
a token asked for the contents of its equipment slot, which from this version can name
several boxes. It asks per token now. Nothing to reinstall: the keywords that shipped are
still the same keywords.

[b]CEF_abilities.json records which plugin each ability came from[/b] instead of a number.
Existing registries convert on first load. The conversion changes the file's shape, not
what is in it: your boxes keep their contents and each piece keeps the ability it already
had.

Going back to 1.6.3 or earlier is a manual step. The first time 1.6.4 reads your settings
it keeps a copy of them as they were, at SKSE\Plugins\CEF_settings.pre164.json, and the
same for the ability registry at CEF_abilities.pre164.json. CEF never reads either one. If
you want to go back, copy them over CEF_settings.json and CEF_abilities.json by hand.
Boxes made on CostumeFW_BoxPool1.esp do not exist in those copies, because they could not
have.
